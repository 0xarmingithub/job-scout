# Voice and tone: plain, specific, honest

## Core stance

- **Sharp.** Every sentence does work. No filler.
- **Honest.** Never claim what is not in the master content file. When a number
  is not available, say so and ask. Do not estimate.
- **Human.** It should sound like an experienced person wrote it, not a tool.
  Keep the technical depth. Do not simplify for a recruiter at the expense of
  the hiring manager who reads closely.
- **Direct.** Lead with the outcome, then the method.
- **Specific.** Names, numbers, tools, scale. "Azure IoT Hub" beats "cloud
  platform". "40 services" beats "many services".

## Bullet formula

Action verb, what, how or with what, quantified outcome or scope.

Good:
- Built a self-service deployment pipeline for 40 internal services, replacing a manual release checklist.
- Cut onboarding time for a new site by writing Python tooling that automates the first-day setup.

Weak:
- Responsible for deployments and ensuring reliability. (no verb, no scope, no outcome)
- Leveraged synergies to spearhead transformative platform solutions. (buzzwords, nothing a reader can check)

## Verbs to rotate

- **Build and design:** Architected, Designed, Built, Engineered, Implemented, Developed, Prototyped, Deployed, Shipped
- **Operate:** Operated, Maintained, Scaled, Migrated, Hardened, Monitored, Automated
- **Lead and influence:** Led, Mentored, Coordinated, Coached, Partnered, Owned, Established
- **Improve:** Reduced, Cut, Eliminated, Streamlined, Optimised, Accelerated, Refactored
- **Analyse:** Analysed, Investigated, Diagnosed, Evaluated, Specified, Authored

Avoid in bullets: Responsible for, Worked on, Helped with, Assisted,
Participated, Involved in, Utilised, Leveraged, Spearheaded, Championed.

## Words and shapes that mark machine-written text

Remove or rewrite on sight.

**Vocabulary:** delve, navigate (as a metaphor), landscape, realm, tapestry,
weave, embark, journey, unleash, unlock, pivotal, paramount, seamless,
cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class, robust, holistic,
synergy, leverage (as a verb), empower, revolutionise, transformative,
passionate, results-oriented, detail-oriented, proven track record, dynamic,
vibrant, in today's fast-paced world, at the forefront of, think outside the
box, hit the ground running.

**Sentence shapes:**
- "It is important to note that..." Delete it and state the thing.
- "It is worth mentioning..." Delete.
- "Not only X but also Y." Use a comma or two sentences.
- "In conclusion" or "Overall" at the end of a bullet. Delete.
- "By doing X, I was able to Y." Flip it: "Did X and got Y."

**Punctuation:**
- No em dashes and no en dashes, anywhere. Use a comma, a full stop,
  parentheses, or a hyphen in compound words.
- No emojis.
- Straight quotes in Markdown and plain text.

**Rhythm:** one list of three per paragraph is fine. Three in a row reads as
machine-made. Hedging words (somewhat, arguably, perhaps, could potentially)
come out unless the claim really is uncertain.

## Tone by surface

| Surface | Tone |
|---------|------|
| CV bullets | Telegraphic. Verb first. Two lines at most. Outcome first when possible. |
| Summary | Two to four lines. Years, top strengths, what you offer. No first-person pronouns. |
| Cover letter | First person throughout. Direct opening ("I am writing to apply for..."). One page. |
| Profile page (LinkedIn or similar) | First person, a little warmer than the CV, still no buzzwords. |
| Interview notes | Shorthand. First person. Informal is fine. |

## First person

- CV and summary: no "I", "me" or "my". Bullets have an implied subject.
- Cover letter: first person is required. A letter written in the CV's
  telegraphic style reads as stiff and third-person.

## Specificity over volume

If a bullet has no noun specific enough to search for (a tool name, a number, a
protocol, a system), it is probably not strong enough yet.

- "data from APIs" becomes "data from REST endpoints and MQTT-connected gateways"
- "large fleet" becomes "1,200 gateways"
- "reduced effort" becomes "cut manual checking by a third"

(Numbers here are examples. Use only your own, from the master content file.)

## When asked to humanize a text

1. Strip the vocabulary above in one pass.
2. Split long compound sentences.
3. Prefer active voice.
4. Remove hedging.
5. Read it aloud. Rewrite any clause that sounds mechanical.
6. Never change a number, a name, a date or a claim.
7. Keep the technical density.

## Honesty rules

- If you want to add a number to strengthen a bullet, stop. Check the master
  content file. If it is not there, write in the notes: "bullet N would benefit
  from a number on X; user input needed".
- If a posting asks for a skill you do not have, do not add it. Play the gap
  down or record it as a real gap.
- "Transferable skills" is a fair framing only when the experience really
  transfers. Do not stretch.
